1. The Mental Model

Ads are a paid-signal amplifier. They don't create demand — they capture it, or bring your product to people who match your buyer profile.

They only work when four things are true:

  1. Someone is already searching for what you sell (search ads) OR matches your buyer profile (social ads).
  2. Your ad message matches their intent.
  3. Your landing page converts the click into a signup.
  4. Your product converts the signup into a paid customer.
The trap — if any of those four is broken, ads amplify the leak, not the growth. Order of operations: fix conversion → then run ads.

The 4-step funnel

Visitor  →  Signup  →  Trial user  →  Paid customer

Every ad campaign should be evaluated against this whole funnel, not just clicks. Optimising for clicks alone is the #1 way technical founders burn ad money.

Rule of thumb math

For a $6/month product:

  • If your CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) is < 3× monthly revenue, you're doing well.
  • Target CAC: < $18 for $6/mo, < $45 for $15/mo, < $240 for $80/mo.
  • Break-even months = CAC / monthly revenue. Aim for < 6 months.

If those numbers don't add up, either your product isn't ready for paid acquisition or you need to push higher-tier plans.

3. The 90-Day Launch Plan

Weeks 1–2 — Instrument everything (no spend)

Before spending a rupee, know your baseline.

  1. Add Google Analytics 4 (if not already done).
  2. Add three events to your app:
    • sign_up — fires on successful account creation.
    • purchase — fires on successful payment with value.
    • cta_click — fires on landing-page primary CTA click.
  3. Set up Google Ads account at ads.google.com. Verify your domain.
  4. Create conversion actions: Signup (primary), Purchase (secondary, with value).
  5. Link Google Ads to GA4 and import GA conversions.
  6. Install LinkedIn Insight Tag in <head>. Enables retargeting.
  7. Use the UTM builder for every ad link: ga-dev-tools.google/campaign-url-builder/

Weeks 3–4 — Google Search Ads only ($150–$250 budget)

Search ads capture existing demand. This is the highest-ROI place to start.

Use only Exact [keyword] and Phrase "keyword" match. Skip Broad match — it wastes money. Full keyword bank is in Appendix A.

Regions: US, UK, India, Canada, Australia, Germany, Netherlands, Singapore.

Landing page: same landing page for now, appended with UTM. You'll build campaign-specific pages in Week 5.

Expected: 40–80 clicks. Even 4–8 signups will tell you if the funnel works.

Weeks 5–6 — Build campaign-specific landing pages

Cold ad traffic converts 2–3× better on a campaign-specific landing page than a general one.

Build one page per major theme:

  • /l/azure-architecture-designer — Azure keywords
  • /l/terraform-azure — Terraform keywords
  • /l/azure-waf-validator — LinkedIn Azure Architect audience

Each page must have: one clear headline (matching ad word-for-word), one product screenshot with 2–3 annotations, three bullets max, one CTA button, and no nav, no footer clutter.

Rule — ad copy and landing page headline must be nearly identical. Google Ads rewards this "message match" with lower CPC.

Weeks 7–8 — Add LinkedIn Sponsored Content ($200/month)

LinkedIn is where Azure Architects and DevOps Engineers hang out professionally. Highest quality traffic; highest cost.

See Appendix B for full targeting. Single-image sponsored post format. Avoid emojis in ads on LinkedIn.

Run for at least 2 weeks before judging. LinkedIn's algorithm needs 14 days of data to optimise.

Weeks 9–12 — Scale or kill

By week 8, you have real data. Use the thresholds in Section 7:

  • Whichever channel wins → double the budget.
  • Whichever loses → kill within 7 days.

Don't try to save a losing campaign with "one more tweak". Kill fast, redirect budget.

Do all of this before spending a rupee on ads.

Analytics + tracking

  • ☐ Google Analytics 4 property created and installed
  • ☐ sign_up event wired in signup success handler
  • ☐ purchase event wired in payment success handler with value and currency
  • ☐ cta_click event on primary landing-page CTAs
  • ☐ Google Ads account created
  • ☐ Domain verified in Google Ads
  • ☐ GA4 linked to Google Ads
  • ☐ GA4 conversions imported to Google Ads
  • ☐ LinkedIn Insight Tag installed in <head> of index.html
  • ☐ LinkedIn Campaign Manager account created
  • ☐ Daily spend caps set on every ad account

Landing pages

  • ☐ At least one campaign-specific landing page (/l/[campaign])
  • ☐ Landing page loads in < 2 seconds (PageSpeed Insights)
  • ☐ Landing page passes Core Web Vitals
  • ☐ Signup form works on the landing page directly (no extra click)
  • ☐ Mobile responsive verified on real device

UTM discipline

Every ad link must carry UTM parameters. Use this exact format:

https://cloudcanvas.co/l/azure-architecture-designer
  ?utm_source=google
  &utm_medium=cpc
  &utm_campaign=azure-tools-exact
  &utm_content=headline-v1
  &utm_term=azure+architecture+designer
  • utm_source — google | linkedin | reddit | twitter
  • utm_medium — cpc | sponsored | display
  • utm_campaign — your campaign identifier
  • utm_content — ad variant (v1, v2, image-a)
  • utm_term — the keyword (search only)

Consistency here is the difference between "we know what works" and "guess and pray".

6. Landing Page Rules

Rules learned the hard way. Every one is enforced on Cloud Canvas Designer.

  1. Message match. Ad headline and landing page H1 must be nearly identical. Google Ads reduces your CPC when it sees alignment.
  2. One goal per page. Campaign landing page has ONE conversion action — sign up. No "learn more", no "book demo".
  3. Strip the nav. Remove header nav on campaign landing pages. Every extra link is an escape route.
  4. Above-the-fold rule. Headline + one-line value + primary CTA visible without scrolling. On mobile too.
  5. Real screenshot > illustration. People trust product screenshots more than illustrations.
  6. Social proof, if you have it. One testimonial with a name and job title beats none. Company logos work too.
  7. Address the objection. For a free trial, address "no credit card" prominently. For paid, "cancel anytime".
  8. Sub-2-second load time. Cold ad traffic has near-zero patience. Preload the hero image.
  9. Mobile-first. 60%+ of B2B ad traffic is mobile. If it looks bad on your phone, redesign it.
  10. Track sign-up on the landing page itself. Don't send users to a separate signup page.

7. Metrics and Decision Thresholds

The only five metrics that matter, and when to kill or scale.

MetricDefinitionKill ifScale if
CPCCost per click> $12< $4
CTRClick-through rate< 1%> 3%
Signup conv.Clicks that become signups< 3%> 8%
CACCost per signup> $50< $15
Trial → paidSignups that upgrade< 3%> 10%

The one metric that overrides everything

CAC : LTV ratio. LTV = average revenue per paid customer over their lifetime.

  • Healthy: LTV / CAC ≥ 3
  • Marginal: LTV / CAC between 1 and 3
  • Losing money: LTV / CAC < 1

Estimate LTV as: monthly revenue × average retention months. For a $6/mo product with 12-month average retention, LTV = $72. CAC must be < $24.

Reporting cadence

  • Daily for the first 2 weeks (catch runaway spend early)
  • Weekly after that (Monday morning check-in)
  • Monthly budget review — kill losers, double winners

8. Common Landmines

Real mistakes technical founders make. Every one costs money.

Landmine 1 — Broad match keywords. Google matches your ad to garbage queries and drains the budget. Use ONLY Exact and Phrase match until you have significant data.
Landmine 2 — No conversion tracking. Google Ads optimises for whatever you tell it. Without signup tracking, it optimises for clicks, not signups.
Landmine 3 — Sending ads to the homepage. Every extra second is a chance to bounce. Ad → focused landing page → signup. That's it.
Landmine 4 — Automated bidding too early. Google's smart bidding needs 50+ conversions. Until then, use Manual CPC with hard caps.
Landmine 5 — No daily budget cap. Set daily maximums at 3× your intended spend as a safety net. A targeting mistake can nuke your bank account in hours.
Landmine 6 — Testing 5 variables at once. Test ONE variable per campaign. Otherwise you learn nothing.
Landmine 7 — Judging results after 3 days. Ad platforms take 2 weeks to learn. Real results start on day 14–21.
Landmine 8 — Optimising for the wrong step. Fixing CPC when signup conversion is the problem is like tuning your database when the bottleneck is the network. Fix the actual bottleneck.
Landmine 9 — Cheap traffic that never converts. $1 CPC on junk queries beats $8 CPC on high-intent — until you realise those users never sign up. Higher intent wins.
Landmine 10 — Forgetting retention. Getting users is only half the equation. If they leave after month 1, no ad campaign can save the business.

9. Budget Guide

Realistic monthly budgets for a solo technical founder.

Month 1 — $300 total (learning phase)

  • $200 Google Search Ads
  • $100 LinkedIn (small test)
  • Goal: 30–50 signups. Measure conversion. Don't optimise yet.

Month 2 — $500 total (if Month 1 showed signups)

  • $300 Google Search (double best-performing keywords)
  • $200 LinkedIn
  • Goal: 60–100 signups. 3–5 paid conversions.

Month 3 — $800 total (only if CAC < $30)

  • $500 Google Search
  • $200 LinkedIn
  • $100 Reddit test
  • Goal: 120–180 signups. 8–15 paid conversions.
Stop-loss rule — if after $500 total spend you have zero paid conversions, STOP. Either the landing page is broken or the product isn't ready for paid acquisition. Don't throw good money at a broken funnel.
Scaling rule — if CAC is under half your target (e.g. target $20, actual $8), you're leaving growth on the table. Increase budget 50% and monitor for a week. Growth compounds only when you scale winners aggressively.

10. Emergency Triage — When Metrics Tank

Symptom: CPC is climbing

  • Check keyword match types — did Google add Broad Match variants?
  • Check Quality Score (Google Ads → Keywords tab). Below 7 = ad and landing page don't match well enough.
  • Add negative keywords to filter irrelevant matches.

Symptom: Clicks up but signups flat

  • Landing page issue. Check bounce rate in GA.
  • Check page speed on PageSpeed Insights.
  • Test signup form on mobile, incognito, real device.
  • Check bot traffic (Google Ads → Reports → Auction Insights).

Symptom: Signups happening but nobody upgrades

  • Trial-to-paid problem — not an ad problem.
  • Interview 5 recent signups: what stopped them from upgrading?
  • Check email deliverability of the drip emails.
  • Consider shortening the trial (7 vs 14 days converts differently).

Symptom: One campaign eats all the budget

  • Check "Search Terms" report in Google Ads.
  • Add negative keywords for irrelevant queries.
  • Split the campaign into tighter ad groups.

Symptom: CAC fine but revenue flat

  • Users are on free trial but not upgrading. Focus on trial conversion.
  • Review pricing tiers — is the paid tier obviously better than the trial?
  • Add friction to the free tier (watermark on exports, cap on cloud saves).

Closing Thought

Paid ads are the fastest way to buy answers to questions you're guessing at:

  • Do people actually search for what I sell? — Google Ads answers this in 2 weeks.
  • Does my landing page convert? — CTR + signup rate answers this in 2 weeks.
  • Is my pricing right? — Trial-to-paid % answers this in a month.

Every dollar spent on ads should return learning + revenue, in that order. If a campaign teaches you nothing and returns nothing, kill it. If it teaches you something (even negatively) and returns some, keep it running while you fix the funnel.

The winning mindset — the founders who succeed with paid ads treat them like a series of controlled experiments, not a magic growth button. Instrument first, spend small, kill fast, scale winners aggressively.
